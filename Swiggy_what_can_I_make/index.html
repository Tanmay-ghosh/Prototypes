What can I cook tonight?

A pantry-aware cooking assistant inside Swiggy Instamart, built for people who cook out of necessity

Product teardown & feature proposal · Tanmay

TL;DR

The whole proposal in three lines

Core strength

Instamart already solves getting ingredients to your door in minutes, and it knows what you have bought.

Biggest gap

Deciding what to cook still happens outside the app, in your head, on YouTube, or in a chatbot.

Recommendation

Turn order history into a pantry, suggest quick recipes from it, and let users buy only what is missing in one tap.

Context: a crowded shelf

Instamart competes in a market where switching apps costs nothing

A close fight for No. 2

Blinkit holds roughly half the market; Instamart and Zepto are neck and neck behind it, so retention matters more than acquisition.

Users multi-home freely

Users switch between apps on offers, stock and delivery time, and Amazon Now and Flipkart Minutes add more choice. There is little lock-in.

Swiggy is already betting on AI

In Jan 2026 Swiggy launched MCP integrations so ChatGPT, Claude and other AI assistants can build Instamart carts, e.g. ingredients for a recipe.

So what?

Speed and price are table stakes that every competitor matches. The durable edge is something rivals cannot copy: a user's own history, turned into a service they would lose by switching.

Sources: Swiggy press release on MCP integrations (Jan 2026); Datum Intelligence share estimates via Reuters (Jan 2026) and 2026 industry reporting.

Who we are building for: the necessity cook

Primary segment, chosen over the cooking enthusiast

Arjun, 26

Software engineer, lives alone in Bengaluru. Cooks most weeknight dinners to save money and eat better than takeaway. Not a foodie: cooking is a chore he wants done well and fast.

Wants

Dinner on the table in about 20–30 minutes, from ingredients he mostly already has.

Struggles with

The daily "what do I make?" decision, and forgetting one ingredient mid-recipe.

Today he…

Stares into the fridge, searches YouTube, then opens Instamart and searches items one by one.

Why not the enthusiast?

Enthusiasts want inspiration and variety; necessity cooks cook more often, so they create more repeat orders.

Persona is a working hypothesis, to be validated through interviews with solo cooks before build.

Where it breaks: The decision (steps 1–3) happens entirely outside Instamart, so Swiggy only enters at the last step, as a commodity supplier. At that point the user can just as easily open Blinkit. Instamart knows what he bought last week but does nothing with it.

What Instamart gets right, and what it misses

Gets right

  • Fast delivery makes a mid-recipe top-up realistic
  • Reorder shortcuts for items bought before
  • Rich order history per user, across months
  • MCP shows Swiggy can already map a recipe to a cart

Leaves on the table

  • No idea what the user still has at home
  • Recipe-to-cart needs an external chatbot and a setup most users will never do
  • Only works in one direction: you must already know the dish
  • Order history is used for reordering, not for helping decide

The insight

The necessity cook doesn't lack recipes. He lacks a fast answer to "what can I make with what I already have?"

Exists today (via MCP)

Recipe → Ingredients → Cart

User must already know the dish, and must leave the app.

Proposed

What I have → What I can cook → What's missing

Starts from the pantry. Works inside Instamart. Answers the real question.

Making the pantry trustworthy

Order history is not inventory, so the model blends four signals

Instamart orders

The base layer: what was bought, and when.

Shelf-life decay

Items fade out over time: milk in days, rice and dal over weeks.

Quick confirm

A 5-second "still have these?" swipe before suggestions refresh.

Add from elsewhere

Manual add or a fridge photo covers Blinkit, Zepto and kirana buys.

Design principle: suggest with confidence, never block. If the pantry is uncertain, show the recipe anyway and flag "you may need…".

Where it lives: earn the tab

A new tab is the end state, not the starting point

Phase 1

Home-screen card

"What can I cook tonight?" on the Instamart home. Zero new navigation to learn.

Phase 1

Post-delivery nudge

"You just got paneer and capsicum. Here are 3 dishes." Catches users at peak intent.

Phase 2

Swiggy AI tab

Once weekly usage proves the habit, graduate to a tab with pantry, plans and history.

Why: the Swiggy app already splits attention across food, groceries, dining and more. A tab is expensive real estate, so the feature should prove a weekly habit before claiming it.
